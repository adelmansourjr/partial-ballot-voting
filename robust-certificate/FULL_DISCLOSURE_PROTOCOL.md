# One-report outcome changes and incentives under full disclosure

Frozen follow-up after the original 90% disclosure test had too few certificates to separate methods. This study targets vulnerability in complete reported profiles and distinguishes any possible outcome change from a change that strictly benefits the voter who makes it. The mathematical audit also constructs a separation for a single rival, which is weaker than separating complete winner certificates.

Use independent new profile seeds with the original six-proposal, three-seat, 36-voter design. Draw 128 clustered profiles at 4201270 and 64 independent profiles at 4202270. For each of the five qualifying archives draw 20 seed-defined projections at 4203270 plus 100 times the pinned source index. Stay with the original ordinal committee tie rule. Run the exact, fractional and global-Lipschitz certificates at both 98% and full disclosure for budgets 0–3. The 98% subset uses the original uniformly shuffled bit mask at each new seed. Every method sees the same reports and follows the same tie-breaking rule. No profile is filtered for a close or convenient majority margin.

For every full profile independently enumerate all 216 possible single-bit ballot alterations and recompute the resulting winning committee. Evaluate the affected voter's utility on their original ballot by counting how many of their sincerely approved candidates win. Score the profile as strategically manipulable at one bit if there is at least one alteration yielding a strict utility gain to that voter. Also count profiles with a utility-improving alteration where both the original and resulting PAV winners have a strictly higher score than every runner-up. This is a restricted manipulation model with known complete sincere ballots and an exhaustive search, not an account of voter incentives under incomplete knowledge. The exact certificate must identify the same set of one-bit outcome vulnerabilities as exhaustive enumeration.

Primary endpoint is the proportion of complete clustered profiles with a distinct winner under at least one allowed single-bit alteration. Report the paired-profile bootstrap interval and the stratification by whether alteration benefits the reporting voter. A practical frequency of interest is 25%. Use 4,000 bootstrap draws with seed 4208850. Secondary comparator is an analytically evaluated audit of 20 randomly chosen distinct bits. Given A outcome-changing bits among 216, its probability of detecting one is 1 - choose(216 - A, 20) / choose(216, 20). Report the expected false-negative rate conditional on a vulnerable profile, rather than one lucky audit seed. Group real ballot outcomes by source and report equal-source averages descriptively. The original ballot authors are not alleged to have manipulated any election.

To separate exact and fractional margins, compare the target ABC and rival DEF in the complete three-row profile ABCDE, ABCDF, ABCEF. Check the two-bit budget by exhaustive enumeration and the fractional bound with rational arithmetic, reporting the gap even if a different rival defeats ABC within the budget. The calculations do not infer a complete-certificate separation from this pair.
