# Notes for a conversation with Martin Bullinger

## Project match

The Bristol project **Algorithms for Collective Decision Making: Voting and Coalition Formation** invites work on voting rules that represent diverse preferences and on cooperation among agents with competing interests. This follow-up takes the voting route and tests the cost of implementing one rule exactly from incomplete input. [Direct project advert](https://www.findaphd.com/phds/project/algorithms-for-collective-decision-making-voting-and-coalition-formation/?p200953) · [FindAPhD listing archive](https://www.findaphd.com/phds/latest/phd-research-projects/keele-university/?43Mw0w00=&Show=M).

## Reading

- **Participation Incentives in Approval-Based Committee Elections**, Bullinger, Dong, Lederer and Mehler, AAAI 2024. Theorem 1 proves group participation for approval-based committee scoring rules. The proof compares the full and reduced electorates' scores. The paper constructs participation failures for many sequential rules and identifies laminar approval sets as one useful restricted domain. This makes the implementation distinction concrete. Querying sequentially can preserve the exact PAV outcome if the stopping condition is exact. Picking members sequentially defines a different rule. [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i9.28810) · [Author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08798).
- **Selecting Interlacing Committees**, Dong, Bullinger, Wąs, Birnbaum and Elkind, AAMAS 2025. Sections 3–5 formalise direct shared-representative connections and connection through chains of represented voters. The authors give tractable algorithms for voter-candidate interval preferences and examine trade-offs with proportionality and diversity. One sharp extension would be to ask how much extra elicitation is needed to certify a PAV-optimal outcome subject to a minimum interlacing constraint. That objective introduces voter interactions and would need a different certificate. [Author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02519).
- **Fair and Efficient Investment in Public Transportation**, Bullinger, Elkind and Köck, 2026 revision. Sections 3–4 make the planning objective explicit in a fixed investment budget, compare utilitarian and egalitarian welfare, show failures of natural greedy approaches, and obtain exact algorithms in restricted regimes. This study borrows that discipline of assessing an exact decision against attractive heuristics. A further connection is to study acquisition of uncertain traveller valuations for a known transit design choice. The present experiments use approvals over equally eligible projects. [October 2026 author revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03687v3).
- **Approval-Based Committee Voting under Incomplete Information**, Imber, Israel, Brill and Kimelfeld, *Artificial Intelligence* 2025. Theorem 9 decomposes worst-case differences for a given candidate committee across voter completions. The experiment uses that result as its theoretical base, derives a bucket calculation when exact per-voter approval totals are known, then measures certificate stopping and query selection on the resulting problem. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2025.104381).
- Bullinger and Niclas Böhmer are editing an ACM special issue on committees, clusters and assemblies. It is a contemporaneous sign that approval-based committee algorithms fit the advertised direction. [Author's publication and supervision page](https://martin-bullinger.github.io/).

## A focused follow-up question

Would you start by characterising how much exact approval totals can reduce worst-case query complexity, or by modelling unequal response costs and abstention so that outcome participation and elicitation burden can be assessed together?

## Show a binary-question example

On a concrete twelve-voter profile the best possible approval-only method requires 36 answers. With approval-count thresholds in the question menu, 24 yes/no answers are optimal. An exact two-step optimiser of the same pressure objective gained little from the mixed interface on 120 fresh clustered profiles, even though the plan can adapt its second action after the first reply. A useful next question is which structural features make threshold information valuable for actual certification and how to detect them before choosing a question.
